-- Artworks --

Ambiguous Size

This is a project I made in Photoshop in roughly 20 minutes. You might be wondering "why would he put this on here if he didn't put any effort into it?" The project was for a competition between the AM and PM class for Digital Media & Design at LCC. We had to make a photoshop composition that represented ambiguous size, and I had no idea what to make. It took me about 40 minutes to an hour to settle with this design, and I'm proud of the thought process that got me to this conclusion. In the image, you are blowing bubbles that have planets or moons inside of them, which puts the planets and moons down to a relatable scale for humans, illustrating ambiguous scale.

David W/ Straight Lines

One project we had to do in Digital Media & Design was studying a famous artist and recreate one of their famous art pieces. I chose Michelangelo's David sculpture. The research and writing assignment took about 2-3 days, and this took 2 days to make. For reference, each day in class was about 2 hours. I didn't know how to recreate David digitally, so I decided to trace prominent lines and hope for the best. Lucky for me, I like the result. I overlayed the straight line design over the original sculpture and put some other elements in the composition to make it feel more complete. And here we are.

Nighttime Road

For the Digital Media & Design class in the EATON Resa program, one of our assignments was to mess around with perspective warping in Photoshop. Our class had to create a Photoshop project that looked like it stretched on into the horizon forever. Originally, I wanted my composition to look more 3D, but in the end it turned out looking like a retro 2D game that has a road stretching out into infinity. And yes, I did make the galaxy in the sky.

Steamfest Poster

Since this project was at the start of the year, my memory is a little hazy when it comes to remembering what I did for it. My class was told to make a poster for the upcoming SteamFest event, and we were given information and guidelines for what to put on the poster. This poster was made at the start of when I was learning how to use photoshop, so I am confident I could make a better piece with the skills I have accumulated now. Disregarding that, I am, and was, happy with how the poster turned out. Although the text in the center is a little hard to read, I do believe I did well with the skillset I had.

Optical Illusion

I think optical illisions are pretty cool so I decided to make one for this portfolio. In this optical illusion, there is a fisheye warp applied to the layers that move, and the column in the middle of the three is facing the opposite way (which is what creates the optical illusion). This is one of two projects made in Photoshop that were needed in this portfolio for full credit. Our class had to create two new art pieces and have 4 old art pieces in this portfolio for full credit. The other one that I had to make is the next piece on this list.
